The plan phase — grill before the artifact, and a register for the record

2026-08-10 · feature: plan-phase (proposed, not yet registered) · reviewed · round 2

TL;DR

Two additions to the harness, both playbooks, both in brain-axi so every repo that installs the brain skill inherits them. brain playbook grill interviews the human before the plan artifact exists, and checks the brain for an already-settled answer before asking anything. brain playbook write gives the written record — checkpoints, run notes, feature docs, commit bodies — a reference register instead of the release-note register it uses today.

Product brief

Problem + evidence

Nothing in the plan phase asks the human a question until the artifact is already written. playbook start frames the task from the user's opening words; playbook plan writes 17 sections; brain review collects reactions. The first time the human is asked to choose anything, the thing has been fully designed.

The state says what that produces. Two checkpoints, verbatim from brain progress:

2026-08-06 — “…planned task-coordination (feat-009) — plan reviewed round 1, all 9 decisions at recommended, tier complete (phases 1-4).”
2026-07-31 — “…reviewed via brain review round 1: all 4 decisions at recommended (GitHub Actions CI, eval kind=judge, model=claude-opus-5, global runs/gates.jsonl).”

Thirteen of thirteen decisions, across two plans, landed on the option the agent had already pre-checked. Both approved in a single round. That is either flawless calibration, or the cards arrive after the thinking is finished and there is nothing left to choose. Grilling first is how you find out which — a question asked before the design exists can still change it.

Second problem: the written record is expensive to read cold. The current top entry in .brain/runs/progress.md in the cf-saas-starter template puts five sentences, four parentheticals and two em-dash asides inside its ## heading — where that file's own documented format says <one-line summary>. The format is right. Nothing enforces it, and no playbook says how to write what goes in it.

User + job

Two users, both real:

  • The agent in the plan phase. It has run playbook start, holds a framed intent, and is about to write an artifact. Its job is to produce a plan the human approves in one round because the plan is right, not because the plan is the only option presented.
  • The agent reading the record cold, six weeks later. It runs brain progress or opens a feature doc looking for one fact — what was decided, what the gate was, what shipped. Its job is scanning, not reading.

Success metric

Share of plan decisions that land somewhere other than the agent's recommendation. Today that is 0 of 13 across the two most recent plans. If grilling works, decisions get settled in conversation before the artifact, so the artifact carries fewer cards and more citations — and the cards that remain are the ones that genuinely could go either way.

Secondary, cheaper to read: rounds-to-approval stays at 1, and no plan regresses to 2+.

Non-goals

  • No new state file. Grill output lands in the plan artifact and the existing reviews.jsonl.
  • Not a replacement for brain review. Grilling happens in the terminal, before the browser.
  • No restyling of the plan artifact's CSS. Section 0 stays as it is.
  • No retroactive rewrite of the 38 existing checkpoints.

Prior art

The interview loop — design tree, frontier, numbered rounds, agent finds facts and human makes decisions — is lifted from mattpocock/skills' grilling skill. The register rules are its writing-for-agents skill, minus the parts that only matter to a plugin distribution. Neither has any state, so neither can do the memory check in §8 D5, which is the part that matters most here.

Product decisions

P1. When is grilling required? settled by precedent

A mandatory interview on a two-line change is ceremony. Optional means it never runs.

Prior decision — product-planning-mockups, 2026-07-30: “When are the product + UX sections required?” → conditional on a real trigger. The recommendation below follows that pattern. Confirm, or reopen.

OptionProsCons
Binds to the real trigger — an unanswered question. Trivial plans skip it for free. The agent judges "will there be cards" before writing them.
No judgement call, no skipping. Ceremony on small plans teaches the agent to rush it.
Zero friction. The human is the index; it will be forgotten exactly when it was needed.

P2. Does playbook write cover commit bodies?

Commit messages are not brain state. brain-axi has no hook there and can never gate it.

OptionProsCons
One standard for the whole written record. Commit bodies have the same defect today. Advice the CLI cannot enforce sits next to advice it can.
Everything in the playbook is gateable. Cleaner boundary. Leaves the most-read artifact in the repo unaddressed.

Technical decisions needed

1. Own playbook, or a phase inside playbook plan? settled by precedent

Prior decision — product-planning-mockups, 2026-07-30: “How do the two capabilities get packaged?” → two new playbooks with thin branches in plan. Same question, already answered. Confirm, or reopen.

OptionProsCons
Reachable on its own (grilling a decision with no plan attached is useful). Keeps plan from growing past 952 lines. One more id in the index the agent must know to reach.
Impossible to skip — you read it on the way to writing. Unreachable when you want the interview without an artifact.

2. Where does grill output land?

OptionProsCons
No new format. The human confirms rather than re-answers. Answers still reach reviews.jsonl through the normal queue. An abandoned grill (no artifact written) leaves no trace.
Survives an abandoned session. Queryable on its own. A second decision store next to reviews.jsonl — two places to look, drift between them.
Zero new machinery. Prose, unstructured — the next agent cannot tell a settled answer from a note.

3. How hard is the “already decided” check?

Before asking anything, grill searches reviews.jsonl decision rounds and the brain docs. What happens on a hit?

OptionProsCons
Directly kills re-litigation, which is brain-axi's stated reason to exist. Human can still reopen in one word. A stale decision gets carried forward if the human skims past the citation.
Nothing goes stale silently. Re-asks settled questions — the exact behaviour the brain exists to stop.
Simplest possible playbook. Throws away the only advantage this has over a stateless grilling skill.

4. What does progress add do with an over-long summary? settled by precedent

progress.md documents <one-line summary>. Today's entries run 5 sentences.

Prior decision — task-coordination, 2026-08-06: “Should closing a task require evidence like brain ship does?” → refuses without it. The house pattern is refuse-at-write. Only the cap number is genuinely open. Confirm, or reopen.

OptionProsCons
Matches how every other brain mutation refuses. The agent self-corrects from the help line, no human involved. Breaks any script piping a long summary in. Cap number is a guess until tried.
Never blocks a checkpoint — losing state is worse than an ugly one. A warning nothing acts on is the current situation with extra output.
Enforcement without a write-path change. Found long after the context to fix it is gone.

5. How is a plan's tier chosen?

Adding a small tier (TL;DR · Context · Decisions · Plan of record · Files) beside the full 17 sections.

OptionProsCons
Visible to the reviewer, who can push back in one annotation. No CLI change. An agent under-scoping to save work is invisible until review.
Not a judgement call. A one-file change can carry the hardest decision in the repo.
One standard, nothing to get wrong. Leaves the “plan is too heavy” complaint unaddressed.

6. Which scope tier ships? surfaced by the grill

Every prior plan in this repo carries this card. This one wrote the tiers into the product brief and forgot to ask.

OptionProsCons
Ships both standards plus the enforcement that makes them brain-axi-shaped. Leaves out the one phase flagged as fragile. “Every card is clickable” stays an unchecked rule for now.
Smallest thing that could work. Standards are the whole value; use them before gating them. Repeats the current failure — a documented rule with nothing enforcing it.
Every rule in the plan is checkable on day one. Phase 4 parses decision cards out of plan HTML; if that is fragile it drags the whole feature.

What the register standard actually says

One rule carries the rest: the record is reference, not release notes. It is read cold, months later, by someone hunting one fact. Facts go in named slots; prose is reserved for the why that no slot can hold.

Today, verbatim from the template's progress.md:

PR #24 squash-merged). The ten .claude/agents/ operators now load under Kimi Code too:
.kimi-code/agents is a symlink onto .claude/agents (git mode 120000), .gitignore shares
that one path only, and harness-check check D2 asserts symlink identity rather than
existence. Discovery path was read out of the kimi binary and confirmed with
'kimi --agent <bogus>', which prints the roster at zero model cost. Greptile pre-PR

Same facts, in slots:

## 2026-08-06 — Kimi Code loads the same sub-agents as Claude
- what: `.kimi-code/agents` is a symlink to `.claude/agents`. Add an agent once, both CLIs see it.
- why symlink, not copy: a copy passes an existence check until someone edits one side.

Roughly 40% of the words, scannable without reading. Note what survives: false green is a good compact term and stays. What goes is the rhythm — an aphorism per paragraph, the load-bearing mechanism buried mid-clause, four parentheticals in five sentences.

The playbook carries a slot skeleton per artifact type — checkpoint, run-note step, feature doc, verification doc, and (pending P2) commit body — plus three prune tests borrowed from writing-for-agents: cut any line the reader already assumes; one meaning, one place; prefer a familiar word over a coined one.

Plan of record

  1. Phase 1 — playbook grill. Acceptance: brain playbook grill prints the full standard; brain playbook lists 9 ids; playbook start's closing step and playbook plan's opening both name it; the memory-check step names the exact commands it runs.
  2. Phase 2 — playbook write. Acceptance: prints the register rule plus a slot skeleton for each artifact type; playbook execute and playbook done point at it where they instruct a write; brain playbook lists 10 ids.
    Why two playbooks and not one “authoring” playbook?

    They fire at different moments and for different readers. Grill runs once, before an artifact exists, and its reader is the human. Write governs every artifact the harness ever produces, and its reader is a future agent. Merging them makes one document that is wrong half the time it is opened.

  3. Phase 3 — tiering + the length gate. Acceptance: playbook plan opens with the tier ladder and the small tier's section list; brain progress add handles an over-long summary per decision 4 with a copy-pasteable help: line; existing checkpoints are untouched.
  4. Phase 4 — check rows. Acceptance: brain check gains a row asserting every decision card in a bound plan carries exactly one (recommended) option; the row passes vacuously for plans with no cards; a planted violation fails it.
    Risk on this phase

    It needs to parse decision cards out of the plan HTML. The review server already audits artifact layout, so the capability exists — but this is the phase most likely to be cut if the parse turns out fragile. Cutting it leaves tiers 1–3 whole.

Testing plan

Open questions

  • Should the memory check get its own verb — brain decisions [<slug>] — rather than grill hand-rolling a search across reviews.jsonl? It would also serve the spec-axis idea (checking a diff against approved decisions), but that is a separate plan.
  • Do grill rounds eventually belong in the brain review browser UI, where the answer is already a click and lands in reviews.jsonl natively? Terminal-first is right for v1; unclear whether the browser version is a real improvement or just more surface.
  • How do we know the register standard worked? Word count per checkpoint is measurable but dumb. Time-to-find-a-fact is the real metric and there is no way to instrument it.
  • Should playbook write apply to the playbooks themselves? plan is 952 lines and this plan proposes adding to it.
